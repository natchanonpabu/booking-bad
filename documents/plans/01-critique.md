I read the full plan (1,827 lines), `booking-screens.json`, and spot-checked the mockup markup. It is a genuinely good document — §0, §3.5, §7's fate column and §13 are better than most production plans. Below is only what is broken.

## Schedule — the estimate is off by roughly 2×, and Gate 1 is the casualty

- **Day 4 is 2.5–3 days of work, not 1.** `SlotCell` + `SelectionBridge` + `CourtMatrix` (two-axis sticky `<table>`) + `LegendBar` + `DateStrip` + `SelectionDrawer` + API wiring. The sticky table alone is a full day: `position:sticky` fails on any ancestor with `overflow:hidden` (both mockup matrix cards have it — 5 occurrences in `2.`, 3 in `_1`), it requires `border-separate; border-spacing:0` because `border-collapse:collapse` drops borders on sticky cells, the corner cell must be sticky on both axes with its own z-index, and **the sticky `<thead>` and the sticky first-column `<th>` live in two different scroll containers** (page-scroll vs the `overflow-x-auto` region). One element cannot stick to both. Nothing in the plan acknowledges this.
- **Day 1 is 2 days** (bespoke SVGO codegen with a typed union and a `-fill` axis, plus tokens, shell, router, asset conversion). **Day 2 is 2 days** (7 data modules including a `useSyncExternalStore` store with sessionStorage + hold sweeping, *and* 9 primitives with real roving-tabindex `SegmentedTabs`, `Toast` live regions, `CopyButton` fallback). **Day 5 is 2 days** (three screens with validation, a radiogroup, a QR, a countdown and five payment states).
- Gate 1 as written needs ~9 developer-days, not 5. Either move Gate 1 to Day 9 and the plan to 16–18 days, or cut to hit Day 5: webfont icons instead of the sprite, 4 primitives instead of 10, no `<table>` (accept the two panes for the demo), a11y deferred to a named follow-up. Do not leave both the date and the scope where they are — Day 3 will get compressed, which is the one thing §15 #3 forbids.

## The a11y construct is wrong in the file the plan calls hardest to retrofit

- **`<table>` + roving tabindex + custom arrow keys is a hybrid that screen readers actively fight.** In a plain data table, VoiceOver and NVDA are in browse mode and *intercept* arrow keys for their own table navigation — the §8.6 key table never fires for the user it was written for. The construct that keeps `<th scope>` associations *and* switches AT to application semantics is `role="grid"` on the table with `role="row"`/`gridcell` and `aria-rowindex`/`aria-colindex`. Also drop `aria-pressed` on 78 cells (VO announces "toggle button, not pressed" 78 times); `aria-selected` on gridcell is the correct affordance and only exists inside `role="grid"`.
- Acceptance item "VoiceOver announces `คอร์ท 3 เวลา 19:00…`" additionally requires the Thai VoiceOver voice to be installed on the test device, and `lang="th"` on the grid container, or it reads as transliterated noise. Worth one line on Day 8 so it isn't discovered at 5pm.

## Real bugs in the code the plan presents as final

- **`addDays` and `dayOfWeek` are timezone-broken.** `new Date('2026-09-11T00:00:00+07:00').getDay()` / `.setDate()` read *runtime-local* components. On any machine west of +07 (a CI runner on UTC, a laptop left on another timezone), `addDays` is off by one and `dayOfWeek` returns Friday for a Saturday — so weekend peak pricing silently mis-computes and test 16 ("Saturday 10:00–12:00 = ฿440") passes or fails by machine. This breaks the showpiece of steps 14–15. `clock.ts` mixes UTC arithmetic (`currentHour`, `toISODate`) with local arithmetic (`addDays`, `dayOfWeek`) in one file. Do all of it in shifted-UTC.
- **Two incompatible types named `Selection`.** `types.ts` §6.1: `{date, courtId, startMinutes, endMinutes}`, no discriminant. §8.1: `{kind:'none'} | {kind:'range', courtId, startHour, endHour}`. The plan then writes `selection.kind` (§2.1, §8.5) and `api.createHold(sel: Selection)` against the other one, and mixes minutes and hours as the unit. This will be discovered on Day 3 and cost a rename across the contract file.
- **`Venue`'s policy fields are literal types** — `slotMinutes: 60; minBookingHours: 1; maxBookingHours: 3; bookingDaysAhead: 14; holdMinutes: 10; cancellationHours: 3`. A second venue with 90-minute blocks or a 2-hour minimum cannot be typed. That directly contradicts the done condition ("`types.ts` merged as-is into Plan 02 without edits") and undercuts the D05 "rates are data" pitch, since the *policy* is still constants. Widen to `number`. Also: no `timezone` field on `Venue` — which is what let the clock bug in.
- `quote()` hardcodes `m += 60` and `/60`, so `slotMinutes` is decorative. Step 15 invites the owner to name their pricing; if they say "we sell 1.5-hour blocks" — common at Thai courts — the demo's best beat becomes a lie.
- Seed bookings' `PEAK_2H()` has no `fee` line while `quote()` always appends one, so `PriceBreakdown` renders a `ฟรีช่วงเปิดตัว` row on new bookings and not on seeded ones. §11 spot-checks won't catch it; the venue owner comparing two cards will.
- `฿440` everywhere but `฿440.00` on the payment hero (§9 S6). Pick one; Thai PromptPay screens conventionally show `440.00`, price grids show `฿440`. Say so in `money.ts`.
- §11.3 hex gate is `--include=*.tsx`, so raw hex in `.ts` (`qr.ts`, `sprite.gen.tsx` is fine, `fixtures.ts`) slips through.

## The state machine is not complete

- **`SlotStatus` includes `'closed'` and no rule covers it.** R1–R3 handle booked/maintenance/past; a `closed` cell falls to R4 and gets selected. Either delete the variant or add R0.
- **`BlockKind` includes `'held'` and `SlotStatus` has no equivalent** — `buildGrid`'s mapping is unspecified, and more importantly **the user's own hold is indistinguishable from someone else's**. When is `createHold` called — on the review CTA, or on entering `/book/pay`? Never stated. Once it fires, §8.4's refetch rule ("if any selected hour is no longer available: `S = none` + `ช่วงเวลาที่เลือกไว้เพิ่งถูกจองไปครับ`") will nuke the user's own selection and accuse them of losing their own slot the moment they press Back from review — which §8.4 also promises will restore the selection. Two rules in the same table contradict each other. Needs `ownHoldId` excluded from the availability projection.
- **No rule for the wall clock crossing an hour boundary with a live selection.** Demo at 18:58, select 19:00–21:00, clock hits 19:00 → the 19:00 row becomes `past` but nothing re-evaluates until a refetch. The selection is carried to review and booked into the past. Related: the plan never defines whether the *current* hour is `past` or still sellable at 19:30 — a real venue decision, not a detail.
- **`Shift+↑` is overloaded and self-contradictory.** It "extends `startHour` back by 1, or shrinks from the bottom when focus is at `endHour-1`". But `Shift+↓` doesn't move focus (§8.6: "focus is never moved programmatically by a tap"), so after two `Shift+↓` presses focus is nowhere near `endHour-1` and the shrink branch is unreachable. Also `Shift+↓` at `endHour === 22` maps to R10 on a cell (h=22) that does not exist — needs an explicit clamp.
- **The model is only correct for `minBookingHours === 1`.** R4/R5/R9/R13 all construct a 1-hour range; with min=2 the dev-mode assertion on invariant 2 fires on every first tap. Fine for this venue, but the file is claimed as the surviving contract.
- Taps while `quoting`, while the grid is in `loading` (cells are pulse blocks), or while in `error` are unspecified.

## Product gaps that will end the meeting, not the sprint

- **Multi-court booking is unrepresentable by construction, and the plan celebrates that.** §8.1: "exactly one court… unrepresentable by construction… nothing to validate." A ก๊วน of 8–12 routinely takes 2–3 courts for the same two hours, and the mockups themselves surface the ask — `_2`'s contact card literally reads `ต้องการจัดก๊วนด่วน หรือเหมาคอร์ท?`. This is the most likely thing the owner asks at step 5, and the plan has made it structurally expensive in the exact type it says will never change. Decide now while it's free: `Selection = { courtIds: CourtId[]; startHour; endHour }`, render one court in the UI, and the contract survives.
- **Recurring / standing bookings (ก๊วนประจำ) are absent and are the biggest single reason this system cannot be adopted as-is.** At most Thai badminton venues the prime evening hours are already sold to regulars on a standing weekly arrangement. A grid that renders those as `เต็มแล้ว` with no way for the venue to manage them means the owner still runs their paper book, and the app's availability is a lie the moment a regular reschedules. There is no prepared answer for this in §13 or §15 #5.
- **Walk-ins and phone bookings entered by staff.** Same problem, faster: the front desk takes a phone booking, writes it in the book, the app never knows, two people show up for คอร์ท 3 at 19:00. The owner will ask this within the first two minutes. §13 says "ของจริงจะอัปเดตตามคิวจริงแบบสด" — that is not an answer, it's a restatement.
- **Nothing in the script or the caveat table addresses "เท่าไหร่ / หักกี่เปอร์เซ็นต์"** — commission vs subscription, and whose bank account PromptPay settles into, and who eats the gateway fee. These kill more Thai SaaS deals than any UI. Prepare a sentence.
- Owner-side view (today's sheet, print, no-shows, deposits) is out of scope correctly, but there is no one-line answer prepared, and §15 #5's generic deflection won't survive it.

## PromptPay, as it actually works in Thailand

- **Cutting the "save QR" button is backwards.** On a phone-only flow, nobody scans their own screen. The Thai muscle memory is: save the QR to the photo gallery → open SCB/K PLUS/Krungthai → scan-from-gallery. The plan cut that as "40 lines to make a file nobody in the demo saves" — it is precisely the thing a venue owner checks, because it's how their existing customers already pay them. Either build it, or add a bank-app deeplink row, or at minimum have a prepared line. Note also the artifact-hosting caveat if this is ever demoed as a published page: `<a download>` is inert in some sandboxes.
- **§11 acceptance contradicts §13.** §11: "The QR **scans on three different phones** and opens the success screen." §13: it encodes a demo URL, and §9 S7 lists an "unknown `:ref` → `ไม่พบการจองนี้`" state. So the scanner's phone opens a not-found screen. Fix the QR target to a static `/qr-demo` page and fix the acceptance line, or the flagship physical moment deflates in front of the owner.

## LINE webview — what will actually bite

- **`line.me/R/share?text=` opened from *inside* the LINE in-app browser frequently no-ops or bounces.** The in-LIFF path is `liff.shareTargetPicker()`. Step 11 is a scripted beat; it will fail in the one environment this ships to. Test it from a real LINE chat on Day 1, not Day 10.
- **The claim "LIFF appends its own query params and a hash route survives them" is asserted, not verified.** `liff.state` redirect handling and fragment-based routes have a history of interacting badly. HashRouter is still the right call for static hosting — but state it as an assumption to verify, not a fact.
- **Removing `user-scalable=no` (correct) introduces iOS input zoom.** Any `<input>` under 16px — the phone field on `/book/review`, the native `<input type="date">` — makes iOS Safari zoom the page and not zoom back. Mandate `font-size: 16px` on all inputs in `index.css`. All 4 grid/checkout mockups ship `maximum-scale=1.0, user-scalable=no`, so this regression arrives the moment the meta is fixed.
- **`<input type="date">` on Thai-locale Android renders a Buddhist-era picker on some OEM skins and Gregorian on iOS** — sitting next to a BE date strip. Visible inconsistency in front of a Thai owner. Use the custom strip plus a simple month sheet, or accept and note it.
- **`backdrop-blur-xl` degrades to transparent on older Android System WebView**, which is exactly the population §4.3 argues Tailwind v4 would exclude. The frosted header becomes text over scrolling content. Wrap in `@supports (backdrop-filter: blur(1px))` with a solid `bg-surface` fallback.
- **`fixed` chrome ignores `.liff-column`.** The header, bottom nav and the selection drawer are `fixed`, so they position against the viewport and will span the full desktop window while content is clamped to 430px — the exact problem `.liff-column` was added to fix, half-solved. Each fixed element needs its own inner `max-w-liff mx-auto`. Also the mockup drawer is `max-w-md` (448px) ≠ `--wc-liff-max-w` (430px): two clamps, visibly misaligned.
- **The grid cannot show 6 courts at once.** `2.`'s geometry — 84 cells at `w-[95px]`, `min-w-[570px]`, plus a `w-16` time column — is 634px on a 390px screen. Half the venue is always off-screen, and inside LINE with its own chrome it's worse. For a *sales* demo, "my six courts on one screen" may matter more than a legible price in each cell. Make cell width a token, try 62px, and decide with the owner in the room.
- **Vertical budget is not checked anywhere.** iPhone SE inside LINE: header 64 + nav 64 + drawer ~96 + LINE chrome ~88 leaves ~350px. The pitch's key beat requires the 19:00 row, the 20:00 row *and* the drawer total visible simultaneously. Add that as a Gate 1 criterion.
- **Replacing `2.`'s `window.scrollTo({top:360})` with "auto-scroll to the first non-past row" actively harms the pitch.** In a morning demo that lands on 09:00 and the owner has to scroll to find the evening. `2.` scrolled to 360px precisely to land on peak. Scroll to the first *peak* row, or to the first available row within the peak band.

## Thai typography — the one thing a Thai product must not get wrong

- **The type scale is M3's Latin ratios applied to Thai and will clip tone marks.** `label-sm 11px/14px`, `body-sm 12px/16px`, `label-md 12px/16px` — Thai stacks two levels above (สระ + วรรณยุกต์: ตื้, เกี๊ยะ) and descends below (ญ ฐ ฎ ฏ). At line-height 1.27–1.33 those clip or collide between lines in Noto Sans Thai. Every one of these sizes is used in the grid, the legend and the chips. Set a Thai floor of ~1.5–1.6 line-height, or split the scale. This is not in the plan at all, and `ds.md` inherited it from generated configs that were never rendered with real Thai copy at small sizes.
- Worth one explicit line: `toLocaleDateString('th-TH')` **already returns Buddhist-era years**. A dev who reaches for `Intl` and then also adds 543 in `thaiDate.ts` produces 3112. Say which one `thaiDate.ts` uses.

## The demo script has two beats that will fail in the room

- **Step 3 asks the owner to book "วันศุกร์" but the fixture is keyed to day offsets, not weekdays.** `D.soldOut = BASE + 3`. Demo on a Tuesday → Friday *is* the sold-out day → you hand over the phone, ask them to book Friday evening, and they land on a sleeping capybara. Step 14 ("swipe to Saturday, point at daytime prices") fails the same way ~2 days in 7. Either say "พรุ่งนี้เย็น" in step 3, or pin `soldOut` to a fixed weekday and rebuild the offsets around it. As written this misfires on roughly a third of possible demo days.
- **Step 15 requires a laptop running the dev server, after you handed them the phone.** Editing `rates.ts` changes nothing on a deployed link on their device. The message is the best one in the pitch — move it into `DemoBar` as a live rate editor writing to the store, which works on the phone in their hand and costs less than the laptop choreography.
- **"Four minutes" is not real.** Step 3 is silent observation, step 8 is "listen and write the answer down." Fifteen steps with a real person is 8–12 minutes. Budget it honestly or the presenter rushes step 7, which is the step the whole plan exists for.

## Over-built for a demo

- **The icon sprite codegen** (`build-icons.mjs`, SVGO, generated union, `-fill` axis) is half a day to save bytes on a demo. A hand-written `Icon.tsx` with ~50 inlined paths and a plain union type gets the identical "typo is a TS error" and offline guarantees in an hour.
- **The 250ms fake quote debounce with a shimmer** makes the demo's best interaction (step 5, "ราคารวมขึ้นเองเลย") feel *slower*. Keep the `quoting` state in the code path, set the delay to 0 in Plan 01.
- **Six query flags** (`?demo ?fast ?error ?conflict ?fail ?reset`) to remember under stage pressure. Fold all of them into `DemoBar`.
- **The full `:root` custom-property mirror** of the palette has no consumer in Plan 01 ("canvas, inline SVG, future Flex-JSON tooling" — none of which exist). Dead weight in the file that is supposed to be the survivor. Same for `line.a11y` and the duplicated `swatch-*` utilities.
- `sweepHolds()` on a 15s interval plus per-read sweeping, for a single-user in-memory demo, is server thinking applied to a laptop.

## Under-built — will have to be thrown away or discovered late

- **There is no LIFF spike at all.** The single largest unknown in this product is whether the flow survives `liff.init()`, the login redirect round-trip, and `shareTargetPicker` — and Plan 02 will now discover all of it with a backend also in flight. Two hours on Day 10: a real LIFF app ID pointed at the static host, `liff.init()` behind `?liff=1`, non-blocking, log what happens. That result is worth more than the sold-out state.
- **No instrumentation.** The demo's #1 stated job is to *learn* (step 3: "watch them, do not coach"), and the only instrument is one person's memory. Twenty lines logging `slot_tap`, `first_selection_ms`, `cta_press`, `abandon_screen` into sessionStorage with a DemoBar dump turns step 3 into data, and it survives to Plan 02.
- **`sessionStorage` is the wrong trade for a demo.** LINE can spawn a fresh webview when the chat is reopened, so the presenter loses the in-progress booking mid-pitch — a worse failure than the benefit it buys ("a second person gets clean state," which `?reset=1` already provides). Use `localStorage` plus an explicit reset.
- Nothing defines what `buildGrid` does with a `held` block, what `Booking` carries for a cancellation reason, or how `availability.ts` decides `past` — three functions in the "survives to Plan 02" column with no spec.
