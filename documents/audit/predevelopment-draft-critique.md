## What's missing or wrong

1. **"Book online, pay at the counter" is never considered — and it deletes the plan's most expensive external clock.** The whole critical path (acquirer KYC, BOT licensing question, EMVCo QR, webhook idempotency, fake-psp, the week-4 gate) exists to move money on day one. Most Thai courts take payment on arrival. If the pilot venue is fine with online booking + cash/QR at the desk (or a ฿100 deposit), D17/D18/D19/D73 all collapse, KYC leaves the critical path entirely, and Slice 1 ships in roughly half the time. The venue interview asks about surfaces, hours and rates but never asks **"how do you take money today, and do you actually want prepayment?"** Add that question to `venue-facts.md` and re-derive the plan from the answer — this is the single cheapest path to the same certainty in the document.

2. **Day-1 email #2 assumes a registered Thai juristic person that nobody has confirmed exists.** Acquirer merchant KYC needs a DBD company certificate, ID cards of directors, a corporate bank account and a VAT position. If the company isn't registered, KYC cannot start on day 1 at all, and Thai company registration is itself 2–4 weeks — which lands *before* the 2–6 weeks the plan is trying to buy. This is an unlisted hard dependency sitting upstream of the plan's own stated root. Check it on day 0, not day 1.

3. **A one-venue funnel with a day-19 deadline is a coin flip, and the plan never builds a pipeline.** W1 targets exactly one venue; the fallback is "stamp everything `assumed`." Yet the exit criteria reference *"a written statement that eight venues declined"* — eight venues appear nowhere in any workstream. Pitch 6–10 from week 1, log every objection, and treat the first signature as the trigger, not the target date.

4. **The Founder/Product lane is the most oversubscribed resource in the plan and is costed at zero.** Weeks 1–4 assign one person: four emails, a business memo with a rate card, a full evening at the front desk, a signed venue-facts sheet, six gang audits, a six-product incumbent scan, the v1 cut line, the PDPA position, three legal screens drafted, the clamp-meter readout, **and** a three-week concierge pilot that requires a human answering LINE within five minutes during evening peak. That is two people. The concierge pilot is described as "฿0" — it costs the founder's evenings for three weeks, which are the same evenings as the venue visits. Either staff it or cut the pilot to one gang for two weeks.

5. **Weeks 2–4 are development, relabeled.** Schema, migrations, tokens, ten components, the LIFF adapter, the webhook harness, the fixture, the walking skeleton — that is Sprint 1 by any honest reading. Saying "development starts Monday of week 5" is rhetoric that will cost credibility with whoever is funding this. The genuinely pre-development work is week 1 only: the emails, the venue signature, the gang audit, the cut line, the Flex spike. **Recommend: relabel W7–W10 as Sprint 1, start it in week 2, and keep the week-4 skeleton as the sprint's exit demo.**

6. **The plan never states when anything ships, and the pilot clock collides with the build clock.** It ends at "development starts week 5" with no estimate beyond it. The register says Slice 1 is 6–8 weeks. So a 3-month pilot agreement signed in week 4 has ~4–6 weeks of actual product in it before it expires. Either negotiate the pilot term to start on go-live, or state the launch date in the agreement.

7. **The largest unstaffed workstream is the ~15–20 net-new screens, and there is no designer.** v1 as scoped requires: home dashboard, booking detail, cancel + refund confirm + refund status, receipt, organizer PromptPay setup (D26), LIFF-outside-LINE fallback, consent, ToS, privacy, four owner-console screens, plus loading/error/empty states across every flow — none of which exist in any of the 48 files. The plan assigns "E2 (frontend/design)" five owner wireframes and calls it done. The one genuinely strong asset in this project is the visual quality; shipping fifteen developer-drawn screens next to eight Stitch-quality ones is how that asset gets destroyed. Name who draws them and budget it.

8. **D60 — there is no home screen — is missing from the plan entirely.** The register rates it a blocker: every one of the 48 flows starts mid-journey, and a LIFF cold-open has nowhere to land. The v1 cut line begins at "LINE login + consent · court profile · booking grid." Add the root screen; it is the first thing every user sees and it has never been designed.

9. **If Spike 1 comes back "no," the plan has no answer for what the product is.** It correctly identifies that P2P slip verification into an account you don't control may be impossible, and correctly refuses to let that gate the booking build. But the business memo, the concierge pilot, the gang audit and the entire wedge narrative all rest on split bill. The fallback offered is "an honour-system tracker with no verification claim" — which is a Google Sheet with nicer typography, and is not obviously a business. Write the one paragraph now: *if verification is impossible and the gang audit shows gangs already settle in two hours, what are we selling?* Answering that in week 1 is free; answering it in week 6 is a pivot.

10. **IoT is over-invested against the plan's own register.** D37 recommends IoT does not ship in v1 at all. The plan nonetheless spends weeks 1–4 on: a clamp meter, an electrician's load calculation, an insurer letter, a fake-relay simulator with injectable faults, a 4-day bench spike, hardware procurement for two courts, and a fail-open proof — described as "~4 hours of work." It is not four hours; it is a procurement thread, a licensed trade, and a counterparty. **Keep the ฿1,500 clamp meter (genuinely cheap, genuinely decisive). Defer the simulator, the bench spike, the electrician and the insurer letter until IoT is greenlit.** That returns roughly a week of E1 to the schema.

11. **The clamp-meter decision rule is not measurable as specified.** It thresholds on *"wasted lighting outside booked hours."* A clamp meter measures total draw. Separating wasted from used requires knowing the actual booking schedule — which the venue keeps on a whiteboard. Either pair the meter with a manual usage log for two weeks, or restate the rule against total lighting spend (e.g. "if the whole lighting bill is under ฿15k/month, a 20% saving cannot fund the install") so the number you can actually get decides it.

12. **`scripts/reconcile.js` as a permanent CI gate is waste.** It diffs the fixture against mockups that the same plan freezes read-only, archives 24 of, and deletes several more. A CI job asserting agreement with artifacts you have formally declared non-authoritative will go red for reasons nobody cares about and be deleted by week 6. Run it **once**, as a throwaway, to generate the decision list — then delete it. Same for `tools/fake-psp`: Omise and 2C2P hand out sandbox keys on signup, and a hand-built fake PSP proves nothing about the integration that actually breaks. And seven workspace packages for one SPA plus one API is monorepo ceremony for a team of two; `apps/liff`, `apps/api`, `packages/shared` is enough. **MockLiff, the raw-body fix, and the webhook replay fixtures are the three pieces of harness that genuinely earn their keep — keep those.**

13. **The week-4 gate may be blocked by the exact clock the day-1 email exists to start.** The walking skeleton requires "PSP sandbox intent → a real scannable EMVCo QR → sandbox webhook." Some Thai acquirers gate sandbox credentials behind a submitted or approved merchant application. The plan simultaneously argues KYC takes 2–6 weeks and assumes sandbox access by week 4. **Add one line to email #2: "can we have sandbox credentials before KYC completes?"** If no, the go/no-go gate needs a different definition.

14. **The booking-reference exit criterion is mathematically impossible to pass.** `WC-YYMM-NNNN` with the stated regex `^WC-\d{4}-[0-9A-HJKMNP-TV-Z]{4,5}$` gives a 4-character space of ~1.05M (5 chars: ~33.5M). The criterion demands **zero collisions over 1,000,000 draws** — at 4 chars that is guaranteed to fail; at 5 chars birthday expectation is ~15,000 collisions. Either make the suffix a per-venue-per-month sequence (which is what `YYMM` implies anyway, capped at 10,000 bookings/month — fine for six courts) and drop the random-collision test, or widen to 8 characters. As written, the gate cannot go green.

15. **The match-schema argument is factually wrong.** The plan claims a BWF 21-point set with a cap at 30 is *"the only rule that produces the 30-28 scoreline"* in `30_4_court_4_victory_summary`. A single set to 30 with no deuce — which is precisely what `4_court_4_live_scoreboard`'s JS implements — produces 30-28 just as readily. The two universes are not reconciled by this observation, and since the plan keeps a `match.format` column with a format-keyed check constraint anyway, it deletes no schema. The register's D40 answer (per-format deuce flag) is the correct one; use that reasoning instead, or the claim will be dismantled in the first review by anyone who plays badminton.

16. **Three operational absences, all cheap, all omitted:** no error tracking or logging (in a LINE webview, a client-side exception is otherwise invisible forever — and the pilot metric "30 bookings in 4 weeks" has no instrumentation specified to measure it); no Postgres backup/restore plan for the only copy of a venue's Friday-night bookings; and no reconciliation path for a **missed** webhook — the plan deliberately tests double-delivery and expiry races but not non-delivery, which is the failure that produces a paid customer standing at a court the system shows as free.

17. **The exit checklist has ~40 items with no hard/soft split, several of them externally blocked.** A signed electrician's calculation, a scanned sandbox QR, a returned vendor answer and a signed venue agreement are not all going to be true on the same Friday. Without a rule, the gate meeting becomes a negotiation. Nominate **six hard gates** (venue facts or labelled assumptions · migration + exclusion-constraint test green · fixture loads · real `getProfile` + `shareTargetPicker` on a phone · one Flex bubble rendered in the Simulator · signed v1 cut line) and demote the remaining ~34 to a punch list.

18. **The one-dev variant contradicts the plan's own operability argument.** W2 states plainly that cutting cash while deferring the owner console "produces a system no venue can operate for a single day." The single-developer section then cuts the owner-console wireframes. So the one-dev path ships something unoperable. If there is one developer, the honest answer is to cut split bill and IoT from v1 entirely and ship booking + the four owner screens — say that.

19. **Legal gaps and a sequencing wobble:** the two-question brief omits the question the pilot agreement actually needs — **who is the data controller for player data, the venue or the platform** — which must be a clause in a contract the plan signs in week 4, before the memo returns. Also missing: whether hosting on Cloud Run/AWS regions outside Thailand raises any cross-border transfer obligation under PDPA.

20. **Minor factual drift in the plan's own citations** (fix before circulating, since the plan's authority rests on its precision): `#606C38` is **153 occurrences across 26 files**, not 148/25. The Material Symbols set is **181** distinct glyphs, not 182. The register is titled "72 open decisions" with IDs running to D78 — the plan calls it "78 rows." And `split_bill_line_share`'s primary CTA is not a `setTimeout` label swap: `#btnShareLine` has **no event listener bound at all**, which is worse than the plan says.
